The novel opens with Erik’s mother’s point of view the night he is born in 1831. She describes the moments before giving birth to her son and the midwife’s reaction to seeing the child. Madeleine, Erik’s mother, is horrified upon seeing her son for the first time and is shocked that her child is so deformed. A priest is then brought there to baptize the child and is forced to give the child a name when Madeleine refuses to give one. The priest named the child after himself. She is then left alone with the child and has thoughts of killing it and herself but, never acts upon these notions. She slowly adjusts to having her deformed son only after making him a mask, his first and possibly only real birthday present. She also focuses on her fall from being the envy of the village. It wasn’t until her only remaining servant, Marie, had pointed it that she realized her newborn son was gifted as he was ringing his bells in a specific rhythm as opposed to at random. It then followed that the dog of the house, Sasha had been attempting to play with the baby that Erik crawled for the first time to the dog and said his first word: Sasha. At three months old, Erik was walking and mimicking every word Madeleine said. She then describes that his development was showing no sign of slowing down and that he could read the Bible perfectly and clearly at the age of four. She also discovered that he loved music by stating, “But music was the keystone of his extraordinary genius. Music welled up from some bottomless pool within him and flowed like a ceaseless fountain through his fingertips, making an instrument of virtually every object that fell into his inventive hands.”

On the evening of Erik’s fifth birthday, he is scarred beyond compare. He had gone to the dinner table without his mask not knowing the reason why he had to wear it. Madeleine shunned him and sent him to put the mask back on before he ate. He refused and asked for two presents: a kiss for now and a kiss for later. She denied him this and he still refused to retrieve his mask. Madeleine then drags him to another room and shows him his face. Erik doesn’t quite understand that it was his own face he was shown and calls it a monster out of fear of the face. Madeleine then tells him that it was his own face that he saw and he is emotionally scarred beyond compare. (I believe this is the reason why he likes the number five, i.e. the fifth cellar being his home and box five being his box.) In his fear he had broken the mirror and the pieces of glass embedded themselves in his hands leaving a physical reminder of his fifth birthday.

Over the years after this, Erik receives tutoring from a man well taught in multiple subjects of the time. Erik develops a fascination with mirrors and desires to learn how to make one. He says it is to make his face disappear.  Later on Madeleine enters into a relationship with the new village doctor. During this time, Erik has mastered ventriloquism and he begins to make an angel statue talk. This causes Madeleine to slowly go insane by caring for the statue as if it were real child. Erik sees this as his only form of receiving affection from his mother. One night during his ninth summer, Erik overhears the doctor ask his mother to marry him and then they would send Erik away. Erik sees this as one of his only opportunities to make his mother happy and plans to run away. It is when they hear a commotion outside that Erik purposefully goes outside to discover that the people of the village had killed his poor Sasha, the only one that showed him true affection. It is while he is retrieving his late pet that he is stabbed. The doctor takes care of his wounds and begs Madeleine to agree to his proposal. Erik then takes this as his sign to leave and disappears into the night of 1840.

Erik’s point of view begins that same night that he had run away in 1840. He is very distraught by the loss of his dear Sasha and feels himself overcome with the lust to kill. He then details how he was kidnapped by gypsies. He had only been looking for food when a man named Javert knocked him out. When he wakes up, he is told Javert will become his “master”. It is during this time that Erik is made into the human oddity show, “The Living Corpse” complete with a coffin. In the beginning, he is just shown without his mask and tortured for disobeying. Over time Erik convinces Javert to allow him to have a dress suit and perform before his mask is removed. He does this within a cage and over time he brings in a lot of money. He also learns more skills such as magic tricks and medicine of his own creation that works wonders. Javert is happy with his money maker but mocks him, calling him a Don Juan. Erik learns to hate the nickname and doesn’t forget it. This continues for three years (the end being 1843). One night after Erik had healed one of the gypsy girls, Javert tries to sexually assault Erik. Erik stabs him before he could do so and finally runs away, disappearing for an entire year.

Giovanni’s point of view begins a year after Erik ran away from Javert in 1844. Giovanni is an Italian architect that lives alone while his young daughter is away at a convent school. He starts his point of view at the end of Erik’s time with him and is recounting his time with Erik. He recalls how he had feared for Erik’s desire to create and that he knew he wasn’t innocent at all despite how much he thought of Erik as a sensitive genius. He recalls the day he met Erik when he had been trespassing on one of his construction sites. Erik had held a knife out pointing it at Giovanni. It was this first encounter that revealed to Giovanni that the thirteen year old had killed before. He also recalls how his wife and himself had three daughters before the final daughter that was Luciana. He tells himself no to think of her but to instead think of “the boy; the boy who should have been my son….” He tells of how he had taken Erik under his wing as a master mason, teaching him everything he knew about architecture. He tells of some occurrences with his daughters and Luciana from when she was young and how her sisters would always follow their mother’s orders. He then returns back to his memories of his time with Erik.

He continues with the summer Luciana came home from the convent school. It had been tough adjusting to having both Erik and Luciana under one roof in the beginning. Luciana had a very unstable personality and this caused Erik to grow easily irritated with her. During that summer, Luciana started to like being home more and stayed after she was supposed to leave. She had felt herself finding fancy in Erik when in reality it was curiosity. She began trying to play housewife to them and continued to try Erik’s patience. It all culminated one night a year later when, after she had already angered him by breaking all of his experiments in his room, Luciana asked Erik to take off his mask. She was determined to make him handsome despite the rumors she heard of him. The three of them were on the rooftop garden when Luciana had asked. Erik refused but Giovanni ordered him to do so. This broke the trust between the two men and Erik’s temper flared. He made a dramatic exposition before he finally removed his mask. Luciana is terrified as Erik tries to pull her closer to look at the horror she so desperately wanted to see. She runs to the edge of the roof where a railing was. The railing failed against her weight and she fell two stories down to her death. That night Erik ran away once again leaving Giovanni in mourning of not only his daughter, but of the son he had loved as his own.

Nadir Khan’s point of view begins four years after we last saw Erik (1850), with Nadir in Persia but is a recollection. Nadir explains that he was sent by the Shah-in-Shah of Persia to find this “miraculous magician who sings like a god and performs wonders beyond all imagination”. Before travelling to find this mysterious man, Nadir remembers his dearest son Reza and curses the day Erik had been born. He then describes how he had traveled to Russia where he found Erik who was making a living out of his magic, ventriloquism, and musical skills. When Nadir tries to approach him, Erik responds in fluent Russian giving Erik yet another skill with language. After some time of convincing, the two travel back to Persia along with Nadir’s servant Darius. During the trip, Nadir contracts a cold and learns of Erik’s medicinal abilities calling him a doctor. They continue their journey and eventually reach Persia.  Erik stays with Nadir and meets Reza. He gives the boy an evaluation without him knowing and tells Nadir that his son’s sight and muscle strength will not return to normal. Nadir is saddened by this and lets Erik miss supper. Erik then makes a music box styled automaton of a Russian fiddler that plays a different song each time and won’t play again until there is an enthusiastic applause. Nadir thinks it is a simple music box but is driven mad when he realizes there is magic at play. He stays with the father and son for a couple days and convinces Reza to behave when he has a fit over Erik leaving him. Nadir is astonished at this.

The two then head to the Shah’s palace where Erik is to be presented in court. Erik then stays in Persia for three years. During the three years, Erik is made into the court assassin along with being the chief architect for the Shah’s new palace, complete with secret passageways for the Shah alone to know about. The khanum, the Shah’s mother, takes great pleasure in the spectacles she forces Erik to provide for her. She has him perform tricks of torture to men and takes a noticeable arousal in this. One of the tortures he devises is a hexagonal room with mirrors for walls, a metal tree in the center with a noose hanging from it, and an overhead view from a balcony. This is one of the khanum’s favourite devises as she relishes in the torture it brings to its victims. She also forces Erik to remove his mask in front of her and some of the girls of the Shah’s harem which frightens the girls but excites the khanum. She continuously made attempts to seduce Erik but refused. Erik also stole from the Shah’s cat while he was there and placed blame on a corrupt member of the Shah’s court. Erik is sent to assassinate this man but is frustrated with the men he brings as they don’t want to comply with his method of death: a scorpion. Over time Erik became too overbearing with his power and the Shah was starting to dislike him.

He also visited Reza again as promised, staying for two months to “paint the child a rainbow” and allowing Nadir to say when to end it as his son would have suffered. When Reza starts suffering too much, Erik makes a vial of liquid that will make it peaceful for him but Nadir can’t bear the thought of using a poison to kill his son saying that he would prefer to let nature take its course. Erik refuses and says that, “Nature is a cruel and unfeeling goddess. Will you abandon your child into her merciless hands?” Nadir becomes emotional at this and can’t do it. Erik then takes it into his own hands and gives the child a peaceful death instead of the miserable one he would have had if Erik not intervened. There is an unspoken tension between Erik and Nadir after this and their brother like bond is almost broken. Erik had also developed an opium addiction during his time in Persia but realized it was hurting his vocal chords so he switched to morphine.

It wasn’t until Erik had been poisoned that he and Nadir realized the Shah was no longer in need of Erik. The Shah had feared he would construct another palace like the one he made for him if he were to be contracted to build another and was paranoid that Erik would still be able to build one even if his eyes were poked out. Nadir felt uncomfortable in doing this and devised a plan in which to smuggle Erik out of Persia. He found a skeletal looking corpse of similar features in which he dressed in Erik’s clothing along with taking Erik’s mask to place on its face. Erik escaped Persia and Nadir presented the false corpse of Erik to the Shah. Before he leaves, Nadir makes Erik promise not to kill for perverted pleasure and to acknowledge the difference between that and self-defense. Erik shakes on this and the plan is fulfilled, thus ending his three year stay in Persia (1853).

Erik’s second point of view begins three years after he had last seen Nadir and is now completely emerged in the architect career (1856). Erik states that he had traveled Europe aimless for the three year interval that was not described. He had begun contracting more for architecture so he could fully appease his need to create. He also began composing an opera, Don Juan Triumphant, which he viewed as his way of becoming cynical. He continued both forms of creativity until he grew bored of building houses and found a sudden desire to return to his original home. Upon arriving at his childhood home, he is met with his old maid from his childhood. It is during this visit that he discovers that his mother never married the doctor that she had been dating. Erik is shocked by this and goes to see his mother’s body. Upon seeing her, he comes to understand her disgust with his face and finds that only now do they share a physical resemblance. He then has flashbacks to his childhood of his mother telling him no and of his memories of Sasha, his poor murdered dog that had been the only one to show him affection.

He then returns his thoughts back to the present and is told that his mother had left him the house. She had left him everything and he felt guilty for the way he had treated her. Erik then notices a newspaper that states “Garnier Wins Commission for Paris Opera House”. This new development angered Erik as he knew that it should have been himself to construct the opera house as he was a true lover of the arts with himself even writing an opera. He then sets to bribe Garnier into allowing him to construct the Paris Opera and gets invited to the man in question’s home. They then begin discussing the opera house and Garnier reveals that he knows Erik’s work and was hoping to work with him on the opera house. Erik agreed to this and they began work on the opera house together. It is only when they discover the water problem that they construct the idea of five cellars, the fifth one holding/containing the water. This construction was completed in 1862 making it nine years that they had worked on the cellars.

Erik reflects on the progress they had made and tells of a secluded place he had made in a dark corner of the base. He also remarks how he had managed to not kill anyone in those nine years of construct. It isn’t until news of the Prussian war with France that construction pauses on the opera house. During this time, Erik sets to work creating his home in the fifth cellar so as to recapture the sense of safety he had felt when living in Giovanni’s basement. One night as the siege continued, Erik came across a small cat whom he named Ayesha. She opened his heart a little as he had set himself into taking care of her. He safeguarded her and the opera house. He went as far as using his signature Punjab lasso to kill a man who had wanted to blow it up. It was because of this that Erik reconstructed the torture chamber he had made for the khanum along with wiring the lake to alert him of any intruders so as to ensure the death of any who entered without his permission.

He then ensues the aid of Jules, a man of whom he had worked with for years, to help him move his mother’s furniture into his new home in the fifth cellar. Jules realizes that he knows too much and acknowledges that Erik is going to kill him. Erik then decides to spare him and help pay for his gifted son’s education. Later in 1875, the opera house was opened to the public as it had been completed. He also felt bad for not having the money he had promised Jules for his silence. He then came up with the idea of his monthly salary of twenty thousand francs from the managers of the opera house. He then concocted the idea of the Opera Ghost.
